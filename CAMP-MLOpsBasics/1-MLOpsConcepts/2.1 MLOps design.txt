8. Business requirements
01:16 - 02:12
Apart from the added value of the machine learning model, we must also consider the business requirements. Especially in the design phase, it is critical to think about the end user of the machine learning model. Let's say we are building a machine learning model that predicts the number of sales of a specific product, such that we can purchase the right amount to put in our shop. The machine learning model will output a predicted amount of sales. We must consider the frequency of the predictions and how fast we need them. We must also evaluate the model's accuracy and whether its results are explainable to non-experts. Transparency is becoming a more and more important factor in machine learning development, since it enables us to find why the model makes its predictions, why it is wrong, and how we can improve the model. These requirements all have an impact on what algorithm we will use.

11. Key metrics

To see if the machine learning lifecycle progresses as expected, it is often wise to track the performance of the model. However, as we’ve seen earlier, the roles involved in MLOps processes are multidisciplinary and thus also have their own way of tracking performance. The data scientist looks at the accuracy of a model, how many times the algorithm is correct.

12. Key metrics
02:48 - 03:00
The subject matter expert is interested in the model's impact on the business, for instance, how their work improves due to the use of machine learning. They are primarily interested in domain-specific metrics.

13. Key metrics
03:00 - 03:17
The business stakeholder is more interested in the monetary value of the model, in how many cases do we actually generate revenue. This is often expressed in money or time. To get the most out of machine learning, we must align the different metrics to make sure everyone is on the same page.
